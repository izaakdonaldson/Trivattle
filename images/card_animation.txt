## Interactive card tilt and holographic reflections

Implement subtle mouse-responsive interactions to make cards feel like physical collectible trading cards.

**The effect should be VERY subtle, smooth, and premium.** Avoid exaggerated 3D rotations, dramatic scaling, or excessive movement.

### 1. Mouse-responsive 3D tilt

When the user hovers over a card:

- The card tilts slightly toward the cursor's position.
- Moving the cursor left/right changes the horizontal tilt.
- Moving the cursor up/down changes the vertical tilt.
- Maximum rotation: approximately **2 degrees on each axis**.
- Use CSS `perspective(1000px)` and `rotateX()` / `rotateY()`.
- Optionally lift the card vertically by 2px when hovered.
- Do not significantly enlarge the card.
- Use smooth interpolation so the card follows the cursor naturally rather than snapping between positions.
- When the cursor leaves, smoothly return the card to its original flat orientation over approximately 250–350ms.

All rarities can have this subtle tilt interaction.

### 2. Mouse-tracking holographic shine

For **Rare, Epic, and Legendary** cards, add a reflective highlight that responds to mouse movement.

The reflection should move naturally across the card as though the user were tilting a physical holographic trading card under a light.

Use a separate, pointer-transparent overlay above the card's background but below its readable text.

The effect differs by rarity:

| Rarity | Interactive reflection |
|---|---|
| Common | Tilt only; no shine |
| Uncommon | Tilt with static green border glow |
| Rare | Subtle blue-white reflective highlight that follows the cursor |
| Epic | Soft iridescent purple, pink, and blue reflection |
| Legendary | Gold-white reflection with very subtle rainbow foil and occasional sparkles |

The highlight should be soft, translucent, and directional rather than a bright opaque glare.

For Legendary cards, existing sparkles can become slightly more visible while hovering, but they should remain small and infrequent.

### 3. Performance and implementation

- Prefer CSS transforms and opacity for smooth GPU-accelerated animation.
- Track pointer position relative to the card and normalize the coordinates.
- Use CSS custom properties for tilt angles and highlight positions.
- Use `requestAnimationFrame` if needed to avoid excessive updates.
- Avoid React state updates on every mouse movement; prefer refs or direct CSS-variable updates.
- Do not trigger layout recalculations unnecessarily.
- Avoid expensive full-card blur or filter animations.
- Enable `will-change: transform` only while interaction is active, where practical.
- Decorative overlays must use `pointer-events: none` so they don't interfere with clicking cards.
- Maintain readable text regardless of the reflection position.

### 4. Accessibility and mobile behavior

- Enable pointer-tracking tilt only on devices with a fine pointer and hover support.
- On touch devices, display the normal card without continuous tilt tracking.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`: disable tilt, moving highlights, and animated sparkles.
- Preserve visible keyboard focus states.
- Do not interfere with clicking a card to open its detailed view.

### Overall design goal

The interaction should feel like gently moving a real trading card in your hand.

**Think premium holographic collectible, not an exaggerated 3D animation.**

At rest, the cards should look almost identical to the existing mockups. The premium effects should reveal themselves primarily when the player interacts with them.

Keep the interaction consistent across the collection, team builder, pack reveals, and battle interfaces. Avoid continuous animation for large grids of cards that aren't being hovered.
